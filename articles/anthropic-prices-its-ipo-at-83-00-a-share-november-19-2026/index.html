Anthropic prices its IPO at $83.00 a share | November 19, 2026

Anthropic, PBC priced its initial public offering of Class A common stock at $83.00 a share on November 18, 2026, according to the company’s official announcement. The shares are scheduled to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19 under the symbol ANT. As of about 8:15 p.m. in Seoul, the U.S. regular session had not yet opened. This note records the priced terms and the related Korea market context. It does not forecast a first trade or offer any investment recommendation.

What was priced

The offering consists of 980 million shares of Class A common stock. Anthropic is selling 956,351,354 of those shares. Certain selling stockholders are selling 23,648,646 shares. The company said it will not receive any proceeds from the selling stockholders’ shares.

A week earlier, on November 10, Anthropic had launched its roadshow with a proposed offering of 825 million shares and an expected price of $70.00 to $78.00 a share. The priced deal is larger, and the $83.00 price sits above that indicated range. The company also granted the underwriters a 30-day option to purchase up to an additional 147 million shares, up from the 123,750,000 share option described in the November 10 announcement.

The offering is expected to close on November 20, 2026, subject to the satisfaction of customary closing conditions. A Nasdaq trader notice, updated on November 11, had already set November 19 as the listing date for Anthropic, PBC Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ANT.

The November 18 pricing announcement did not state a final dollar total for proceeds or a valuation. A Yonhap report published on November 19 put proceeds from the basic offering at $68 billion, and at $94 billion when a private placement is included. Those totals line up with the earlier 825 million share count at $83.00, plus a $26.0 billion concurrent private placement of Class N common stock. That private placement was disclosed in Amendment No. 3 to Anthropic’s Form S-1, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 17. The amendment, dated before the share increase, said Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google LLC, Micron Technology, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and TSMC Partners, Ltd. had agreed to buy an aggregate of $26.0 billion of Class N shares at the IPO price, scheduled to close immediately after the public offering if conditions are met. A final prospectus with updated net proceeds after the upsizing was not retrieved for this note. The $68 billion and $94 billion figures should not be read as the official sized total.

A Securities and Exchange Commission filing on November 18 under Rule 462(b) registered 178,250,000 additional Class A shares, including shares subject to the underwriters’ option, at $83.00 a share. That price matches the company announcement. The registration statement on Form S-1, file number 333-299633, was declared effective on November 18.

Why it matters

The listing comes as investors, in Yonhap’s account of the Seoul close, are looking for further evidence that heavy spending on artificial intelligence will produce sustainable returns. Anthropic’s November 17 registration amendment explains how the company said it would use the cash. It intends to use net proceeds from the offering and the concurrent private placement for general corporate purposes, including working capital, operating expenses, and capital expenditures, including investments in compute and infrastructure, as well as hiring and retention.

The same filing said that, as of and subsequent to September 30, 2026, Anthropic had entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements, including short-term commitments and leases not yet commenced, with aggregate committed spend of approximately $900.9 billion, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure. Of that amount, approximately $451.6 billion was expected to be incurred before December 31, 2030, and substantially all of the remainder between 2031 and 2045. Approximately $70.7 billion of the commitments were due in the 12 months following September 30, 2026. Those figures are company disclosures of contractual commitments over many years. They are not a forecast of cash spending in any single year.

The amendment and the company’s IPO site also reported revenue of $17.0 billion for the three months ended September 30, 2026. Park Jae-hwan, an analyst at Eugene Investment and Securities, told Yonhap that Anthropic sits at the end of the AI infrastructure demand chain and could serve as an indicator of future AI demand. That is an analyst view, not a company statement.

Korea bridge

South Korean shares closed higher on November 19, before the U.S. listing open. Yonhap reported that the KOSPI finished at 5,137.89, up 70.45 points, or 1.39 percent. Samsung Electronics rose 1.13 percent to 196,900 won. SK hynix rose 3.41 percent to 1,183,000 won. Foreign investors were net sellers of 405.46 billion won, while institutions were net buyers of 19.22 billion won and individuals were net sellers of 65.2 billion won. Some local market reports put foreign net selling near 403.5 billion won, close to but not identical to Yonhap’s figure. This note does not attribute those single-day moves solely to the IPO, and it does not make any claim about Korean memory or high-bandwidth memory exposure beyond the closing prices Yonhap reported.

In a separate Korean-language Yonhap report issued at 5:12 p.m. KST, market sources were cited as saying that demand to raise cash ahead of the Anthropic subscription was among the factors in recent declines in U.S. technology stocks and the Korean market, alongside other news. Ha Jang-kwon, a researcher at Hanwha Investment and Securities, told Yonhap that the supply burden from an IPO of this scale is typically heaviest in the book-building period before listing. Those comments are analyst and market views. They are not confirmed capital-flow data showing that specific Korean shares were sold to fund IPO subscriptions.

What to watch next

The first item is mechanical. U.S. regular trading in ANT is scheduled to begin at 9:30 a.m. in New York, which is 11:30 p.m. KST on November 19. This note, written before that open, has no opening print to report and offers no view on how the shares may trade. The offering is then expected to close on November 20, subject to customary conditions. The underwriters’ 30-day option covering up to 147 million additional shares remains outstanding. The concurrent private placement, if it closes as described in the November 17 amendment, is scheduled to settle immediately after the public offering.

For Korea, the next observable facts are the usual ones after the U.S. session: the November 20 Seoul open, and any official update on foreign net buying or selling. Those prints should be read as they are released, not assumed in advance.
