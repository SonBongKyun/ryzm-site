Goldman’s Korea Weekly: KOSPI Drops 7.9% as AI Doubts Outweigh the China Deal Rally | November 21, 2026

Source note: Based on Goldman Sachs' Korea equity strategy weekly, "Korea Weekly Kickstart" (November 20, 2026), as relayed in secondary coverage. Targets and forecasts are Goldman's, not RYZM's. This is a summary, not investment advice or a market call.

The KOSPI fell 7.9% this week to close at 4,869.96. The KOSDAQ lost 7.7% and MSCI Korea dropped 8.8%. Goldman Sachs cut its 12-month KOSPI target to 7,500 from 9,000, citing high volatility and slowing upward earnings revisions. The bank kept its view that Korea has the most upside in Asia.

Goldman says its new target implies 4.9 times forward earnings and 1.18 times book value, against a 24% return on equity. Based on the historical link between price-to-book and ROE, the bank says the market would still be more than 40% undervalued even at 7,500. It explains the cut this way: when high volatility and slowing earnings momentum overlap, valuations can stay below what profitability would justify.

The week in markets

Lingering doubts about AI drove the selloff. K-culture stocks rallied on the Korea-China deal, with leisure up 13.1% and food and beverage up 1.9%. Pharma held up better than the market, falling only 2.3%. Machinery (−12.4%), telecom (−12.3%) and construction (−10.2%) were the biggest decliners. Goldman's report also shows Taiwan with the steepest weekly drop in Asia.

Flows

Foreign investors sold a net ₩4.556 trillion of KOSPI stocks, ₩3.639 trillion of it in tech. Retail investors bought a net ₩2.661 trillion, and pension funds sold ₩500 billion.

Rates, FX and risk appetite

USD/KRW was little changed at 1,336. The 3-year KTB yield fell 12bp to 3.64%, and the 10-year fell 14bp to 3.86%. KOSPI 12-month forward EPS rose 0.6%. Construction led with +3.4%, and utilities were the weakest at −7.2%. Goldman's Korea equity risk appetite indicator sat at −0.9, still in risk-off territory.

Semiconductors: how much is priced in

On a three-year-forward basis, KOSPI semiconductors trade at about 0.7 times book, near historical lows. Goldman notes that this level has lined up with past cycle troughs. Trailing price-to-book is 2.2 times, against a consensus forward ROE of 48%. By the historical relationship, a 48% ROE would map to about 5.3 times book. That means current prices reflect an ROE of roughly 20%. Goldman says forward ROE explains about 70% of the variation in trailing price-to-book.

Undervaluation beyond chips

More than 75% of KOSPI stocks trade below book value. The share of stocks yielding over 4% has grown and is now higher than in developed markets.

Governance and buybacks

Korea's score in the Asian Corporate Governance Association ranking rose 5.7 points, the biggest improvement in Asia. That moved Korea from 8th to joint 5th. Mandatory cancellation of treasury shares has made 2026 a record year for cancellations, yet treasury shares worth 2.5% of market cap remain. Goldman expects 2027 buybacks to exceed $130 billion, driven by shareholder-return policies, the Value-up program and employee compensation.

Foreign ownership is lower than it looks

Foreign ownership of the KOSPI is 38%, but the big memory names lift that figure through their index weight. Foreign ownership of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix is 47%. That is 1 standard deviation below its post-2000 average and 2.7 standard deviations below its 10-year average.

Positioning

October preliminary data show Asia funds still underweight Korea at −15bp versus benchmark, after adding 45bp during the month. Emerging-market funds are underweight at −115bp, up 15bp. Korean tech hardware and semiconductors remain underweight at −110bp, while Korean industrials gained weight over the past one and three months.

Hedge funds sold Korea heavily in November, with Taiwan and Korea leading the selling. Net allocation to Korea is 2.3%, the 11th percentile over one year. Net leverage among Asia fundamental long/short managers fell 47.4 percentage points in a month to 10%, the lowest in more than a decade of data.

Valuation

The KOSPI trades at 3.5 times 12-month forward earnings, the lowest since 2008. Tech trades at 2.7 times. For 2027, MSCI Korea is at 3.1 times earnings and 1.0 times book, with a 39.2% ROE and a 5.4% dividend yield. That compares with 13.3 times for Taiwan, 13.5 times for Japan and 19.8 times for India. MSCI Korea's forward P/E is at a 70% discount to MSCI World and a 53% discount to Asia ex-Japan. Korean tech trades at a 76% discount to world tech.

Goldman's macro forecasts

Goldman forecasts Korean GDP growth of 3.8% in 2026, 3.2% in 2027 and 2.5% in 2028, with 2027 inflation at 2.1%. It expects the BOK base rate to rise from 3.00% to 3.50% by end-2027. It sees USD/KRW at 1,298 by end-2027 and 1,253 by end-2028.

Korea bridge

This week ran through three familiar channels: foreign selling concentrated in tech, a stable won and lower KTB yields. The variables to watch are foreign ownership of the memory names, the KTB curve, and USD/KRW against the BOK path Goldman lays out.
