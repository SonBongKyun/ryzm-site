How to Read a Bank of Korea Decision: Policy Rates, Financial Stability, and the Won

A Bank of Korea (BOK) rate decision is rarely a single-variable call on headline inflation. The Monetary Policy Board weighs price stability against financial stability, mainly household leverage and Seoul housing, while the US policy rate sets the cost of dollar funding and FX hedging. So Korea Treasury Bond (KTB) yields and USD/KRW often move less on the decision itself than on the vote split, the three-month conditional rate outlook and the revisions to the quarterly Economic Outlook.

Data as of November 24, 2026 (KST). Figures cite official releases or the named market source. This is a reading framework, not a forecast or a trade signal.

Why doesn’t the BOK set rates on headline inflation alone?

The BOK’s mandate is price stability, with attention to financial stability. In practice the board works under two constraints, and inflation and household leverage do not always point the same way.

The policy path into the November 26, 2026 meeting: after cutting the Base Rate to 2.50% in May 2025, the board hiked 25 basis points to 2.75% on July 16, 2026 and to 3.00% on August 27, 2026, then held at 3.00% on October 22, 2026 to assess the effect of the two hikes (Bank of Korea Base Rate history; Bank of Korea Monetary Policy Decision, October 22, 2026).

The two domestic constraints:

  • Inflation is easing at the headline, core is not. Consumer prices rose 2.8% year on year in October 2026 (+0.2% month on month), down from 2.9% in September as farm prices fell. Core inflation, excluding food and energy, was also 2.8% (+0.4% month on month), well above the 2.0% target (Ministry of Data and Statistics, Consumer Price Index in October 2026, November 3, 2026).
  • Household credit growth is slowing, the stock is not shrinking. Preliminary BOK data put household credit at a record ₩2,030.5 trillion at the end of Q3 2026, up ₩9.8 trillion on the quarter, the smallest gain since Q1 2025. Household loans rose ₩4.7 trillion to ₩1,896.8 trillion and sales credit rose ₩5.0 trillion to ₩133.7 trillion. Inside loans, housing-related lending still grew ₩9.6 trillion while other loans, including unsecured and margin lending, fell ₩4.9 trillion (Bank of Korea, Household Credits in Q3 2026 (preliminary), November 18, 2026).

That balance sheet is why an equity selloff alone rarely moves the BOK. The KOSPI fell 5.21% to 4,869.96 on November 20, with sell-side sidecars on both boards. The policy response on November 22 was a stock-market stabilization fund and looser buyback limits, not a rate signal (see our Korea Close for November 20 and Daily Brief for November 23). Korean household wealth sits mostly in housing, not equities, so the board’s financial-stability lens tracks apartment prices and mortgage growth first (The BOK’s Second Job).

How does the BOK-Fed rate gap reach USD/KRW and KTB yields?

Domestic constraints set the BOK’s bias. US rates and dollar funding set the outer boundary. Since October 29, 2026 the Fed’s target range has been 3.75% to 4.00%, with interest on reserve balances at 3.90% and the overnight reverse repo rate at 3.75% (Federal Reserve implementation note, October 28, 2026). Against a 3.00% Base Rate, the top of the Fed range is 100 basis points higher, or 87.5 basis points at the midpoint.

The gap reaches Korean markets through two channels.

  1. Front end and hedging. On November 20, 2026 the US Treasury par curve closed at 4.63% (2-year), 4.65% (3-year) and 4.92% (10-year) (US Treasury daily par yield curve). The same day in Seoul, the 3-year KTB closed at 3.642% and the 10-year at 3.857% (Yonhap closing table, cited in our Korea Close, November 20). That puts Treasuries about 101 basis points above KTBs at 3 years and 106 basis points above at 10 years. The 3-year KTB itself sits about 64 basis points above the Base Rate, so the front end already prices more tightening or a higher terminal rate. Any change in the expected won rate path moves the rate differential inside FX swap points and dollar hedging costs (FX hedging costs).
  2. Trade flows against capital flows. Rate differentials do not set spot USD/KRW on their own. As laid out in our dollar-liquidity framework, Korea’s chip-led current account surplus, about $340.7 billion from January to September 2026 (Bank of Korea, preliminary balance of payments), supplies dollars to the onshore market, while residents’ overseas portfolio investment and foreign equity selling pull dollars back out. On November 20, USD/KRW closed at 1,336.2 at the 15:30 Seoul close, 6.2 won weaker on the day (Yonhap, cited in our Korea Close, November 20).

A BOK decision moves the won leg of the rate differential and the forward curve right away. Its effect on spot USD/KRW depends on which is pulling harder that day: Treasury yields, the dollar swap basis, or equity and trade flows.

Four things to read on decision day

At the quarterly outlook meetings (February, May, August and November), the rate is only the first line. Most of the repricing risk sits in four places.

  1. The vote split. A unanimous decision signals comfort with the current setting. One or two dissents, for a hike during a hold or for a pause during a hike, tend to reprice the 3-year KTB at once because they show how close the board is to its next move.
  2. The three-month conditional outlook. At the press conference the Governor says how many of the six other board members see room to hold, hike or cut over the next three months under current assumptions. With the 3-year KTB already well above 3.00%, a change in that count often matters more than the decision.
  3. Outlook revisions. On November 26 the BOK revises its August 2026 projections: real GDP growth of 3.3% in 2026 and 2.9% in 2027, headline CPI of 2.7% and 2.3%, and core CPI of 2.5% in both years (Bank of Korea, Economic Outlook, August 27, 2026). Read growth and inflation together. A chip-driven growth upgrade with sticky core CPI narrows the room to pause. Softer inflation with weaker domestic demand widens it.
  4. Financial-stability wording. Watch how the statement describes the Q3 slowdown in household credit (+₩9.8 trillion) against continued housing-related borrowing (+₩9.6 trillion) and Seoul apartment prices. If the board calls the slowdown temporary or not enough, the financial-stability constraint still binds even if headline CPI cools.

How the market tells you which signal mattered

Do not guess the outcome in advance. Read the joint move in the 3-year KTB, the 3s10s KTB curve and USD/KRW after the statement and the press conference.

Market pattern after the BOK What is being priced Channel in control
KTB 3Y up, 3s10s flatter, USD/KRW down Hawkish repricing: a higher terminal rate or more hike-leaning guidance than expected Domestic policy path
KTB 3Y down, 3s10s steeper, USD/KRW up Dovish shift: the front end prices out hikes while the long end follows Treasuries and term premium Wider US-Korea front-end gap
KTB 3Y and 10Y down, USD/KRW up Risk-off: global equity or tech stress outweighs domestic tightening risk (the November 20 pattern) Global risk appetite and equity outflows
KTB curve flat on the day, FX forward points move first Rate broadly as priced; pressure is in year-end dollar balance sheets and the cross-currency basis Dollar funding (SOFR vs IORB)

A falling 3-year KTB with a stronger won points to easing domestic inflation pressure. A falling 3-year KTB with a weaker won usually means external risk aversion is doing the work.

What to watch over the next two weeks

Each meeting sits inside a run of US and Korean data that resets both sides of the spread.

  • November 25, 22:30 KST. US Q3 GDP (second estimate) and October personal income and outlays, including PCE (BEA release schedule). Resets US growth and inflation expectations hours before the BOK meets.
  • November 26. BOK Monetary Policy Board decision and November Economic Outlook (BOK meeting schedule). Check the vote split, the three-month guidance and the revisions to 3.3% growth and 2.7% CPI for 2026.
  • December 1. Korea November exports and imports (Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources). Tests whether chip export receipts still offset equity outflows.
  • December 2, 08:00 KST. Korea November CPI (Ministry of Data and Statistics release schedule). First check on whether core CPI (2.8% in October) starts to follow the headline lower.
  • December 4, 22:30 KST. US November employment report (BLS release schedule). Sets the dollar side of the differential ahead of the Fed.
  • December 8. BOK October balance of payments (BOK statistical release calendar). Shows whether residents’ overseas portfolio outflows absorbed the goods surplus.
  • December 10, 04:00 KST. FOMC decision and Summary of Economic Projections, December 8-9 meeting (FOMC calendar). Resets the US rate path and the longer-run neutral rate into 2027.
