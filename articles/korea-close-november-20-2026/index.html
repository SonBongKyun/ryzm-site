Nov 20, 2026 · Korea Close

Korea Close | November 20, 2026

The KOSPI fell 5.21 percent to 4,869.96, its lowest close since January 16. Foreigners and institutions sold cash shares, the won weakened at the Seoul fix, and Korean government bond yields declined.

South Korea’s stock market finished Friday, November 20, 2026, with losses of more than 5 percent on both boards. The KOSPI closed at 4,869.96, down 267.93 points, or 5.21 percent, from 5,137.89 on November 19. The KOSDAQ closed at 645.68, down 39.13 points, or 5.71 percent. Chip shares led the move after a weak close in New York on November 19, and Anthropic’s first trading session did not lift the wider AI-linked complex.

The close

KTB 3-year 3.642 percent, down 7.4 basis points. KTB 10-year 3.857 percent, down 8.5 basis points, per Yonhap’s closing table issued at 4:40 p.m. KST.

USD/KRW 1,336.2 at the 15:30 Seoul close, 6.2 won weaker than the previous session, according to Yonhap and the Seoul Money Brokerage 15:30 reference rate of 1,336.20.

KOSPI 4,869.96, down 267.93 points, or 5.21 percent.

KOSDAQ 645.68, down 39.13 points, or 5.71 percent.

KRX cash-market flows as of 15:35 KST, compiled by the Korea Exchange and carried by Yonhap: foreigners net sold 1,141.1 billion won of KOSPI shares, institutions net sold 951.3 billion, and individuals net bought 1,595.0 billion. Other corporations were net buyers of 488.3 billion won. On the KOSDAQ, foreigners net sold 173.2 billion won, institutions net sold 107.9 billion, and individuals net bought 291.3 billion. Those figures are cash-market totals. They do not include the alternative venue NextTrade.

What changed

On November 19 in New York, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2.19 percent to 49,097.06, the S&P 500 fell 2.02 percent to 7,175.83, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.25 percent to 23,879.20. Anthropic closed at $97.17, up 17.07 percent from its $83.00 offering price and below its $111.50 open.

Samsung Electronics closed at 183,100 won, down 7.01 percent. SK hynix closed at 1,113,000 won, down 5.92 percent. Hyundai Motor fell 4.67 percent to 255,000 won. Hanwha Aerospace fell 4.12 percent to 605,000 won. Yonhap said every KOSPI sector finished lower except electric and gas, which rose 0.42 percent. Machinery and equipment lost 7.86 percent, telecom 7.78 percent, and electrical and electronics 6.57 percent.

The Korea Exchange paused program sell orders for five minutes on the KOSPI at 11:54 a.m. KST and on the KOSDAQ at about 1:28 p.m. Those curbs, and the index levels around them, were covered in a separate midday note. This report records the 15:30 close. The KOSPI opened at 4,928.51, down 4.08 percent, and traded as low as 4,831.69, down 5.96 percent, before cutting part of that loss into the close. Yonhap said the settlement was the lowest since January 16, when the index finished at 4,840.74, and that the week’s decline came to 7.9 percent.

Where the signal stops

On the KOSPI, decliners outnumbered advancers 805 to 88, Yonhap reported. On the KOSDAQ, Seoul Economic Daily counted 1,509 stocks down and 194 up. Foreigners have now sold KOSPI cash shares for six straight sessions. Yonhap put that streak at 5,096.5 billion won since November 13. In KOSPI 200 futures, a separate book from the cash market, Yonhap said foreigners were net buyers of 92.0 billion won, while institutions and individuals were net sellers of 18.0 billion and 70.0 billion.

Bond yields fell as stocks dropped. Yonhap said foreigners bought 12,541 contracts of three-year Treasury futures and 256 contracts of ten-year futures.

RYZM’s reading: The cash equity close was broad and professionally sold. The weaker won at the 15:30 fix, and the bid in government bonds and bond futures, was the other side of that risk reduction.

Global link

The Korea close followed the November 19 drop in U.S. indexes and in AI-linked shares, including a 2.42 percent decline in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, Yonhap reported. That is a reading of the prior U.S. session, not a view on the next one.
