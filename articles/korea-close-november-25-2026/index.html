Korea Close | November 25, 2026

The KOSPI fell 6.16 percent and handed back the whole November 24 rally in a single session. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix lost about a tenth of their value as memory oversupply worries came back.

Seoul's rebound lasted one day. On Wednesday, November 25, 2026, the KOSPI closed at 4,697.51. That wiped out the 297.49 point gain from November 24 and marked its lowest close since January 13. The KOSDAQ held up better. Government bonds rallied the day before the Bank of Korea's rate decision, and the won barely moved.

The close

KTB 3-year 3.583 percent, down 5.7 basis points. KTB 10-year 3.761 percent, down 5.8 basis points. Both are from Yonhap's closing bond table issued at 4:40 p.m. KST, and the 3-year matches the Bank of Korea's ECOS daily close.

USD/KRW 1,343.2 at the 15:30 Seoul close, 0.8 won weaker than the previous session's 1,342.4. The session high was 1,349.0 at about 9:29 a.m. KST.

KOSPI 4,697.51, down 308.52 points, or 6.16 percent.

KOSDAQ 650.64, down 15.24 points, or 2.29 percent.

KRX cash market, KOSPI: foreigners net sold about 1.90 trillion won and individuals net bought about 1.42 trillion won, per the closing reports from Yonhap and Yonhap Infomax. Yonhap called it the largest foreign net sale since November 6. Institutions ended near flat. Yonhap's Korean and English reports disagree on the size, so we leave that figure out because we could not verify it. None of these numbers include NextTrade.

What changed

The index opened 3.94 percent lower at 4,809.00. At 9:08 a.m. KST the Korea Exchange halted program selling for five minutes. It was the main board's 33rd sell-side sidecar of the year, Yonhap said, and the fourth straight session with a sidecar of either kind. The low came in the afternoon at 4,672.55, down 6.66 percent.

Two stocks did most of the damage. Samsung Electronics fell 9.87 percent to 170,700 won, its lowest close since March 31, and its market value slipped under 1,000 trillion won to 997.96 trillion. SK hynix dropped 11.05 percent to 1,030,000 won, its lowest since April 10. By Yonhap Infomax's count, the pair made up 89.9 percent of the KOSPI 200's point loss. Together they are 44.85 percent of KOSPI market value, Yonhap said. SK Square fell 11.19 percent and Samsung Electro-Mechanics 12.00 percent. The electrical and electronics sector lost 9.81 percent.

The selling started in New York on November 24. BNP Paribas cut Micron to neutral and warned of memory oversupply. Goldman Sachs had already cut its price targets on both Korean chipmakers in a weekend report and sees the DRAM supply deficit narrowing from 5.1 percent in 2027 to 1.2 percent in 2028, The Korea Times reported. Yonhap also cited talk of new U.S. tariffs. The U.S. Trade Representative is expected to publish findings of an overcapacity probe covering 16 economies, Korea among them, with no date set.

Where the signal stops

Not everything sold. Pharmaceuticals rose 4.66 percent. Samsung Biologics gained 5.66 percent and Celltrion 3.83 percent. Cosmax jumped 12.18 percent, and food and tobacco added 2.22 percent. Decliners still beat gainers 593 to 294, Yonhap reported. On the KOSDAQ, chip equipment names took the worst of it. Jusung Engineering fell 11.42 percent.

In currencies, Yonhap pointed to dollar demand ahead of the Samsung Electronics dividend payment on November 27. Exporter month-end dollar selling near 1,350 capped the move.

RYZM's reading: Bonds drew the steadiest demand. The 3-year and 10-year yields both fell nearly 6 basis points a day before a meeting where markets expect a hike. The won took a 1.90 trillion won foreign equity exit and moved less than 1 won. The stress landed on equities, and mostly on two chipmakers and the names tied to them, while drugmakers and consumer names rose.

Global link

On November 24 in New York, the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48 percent and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 7.88 percent. Nvidia fell 5.86 percent, Micron 11.01 percent and the SK hynix ADR 9.9 percent.

Next

November 25, 8:30 a.m. ET (10:30 p.m. KST): the Bureau of Economic Analysis releases October personal income and outlays, including the PCE price index, and the second estimate of third-quarter GDP.

November 26: the Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Board rate decision, with the policy rate at 3.00 percent going in. Yonhap Infomax reported that the market expects a 25 basis point hike to 3.25 percent, with a revised economic outlook. The December bond issuance plan follows. U.S. markets are closed for Thanksgiving.

November 27: Samsung Electronics pays its dividend. U.S. stock markets close early at 1 p.m. ET.

December 1, 11:00 a.m. KST: Korea's November trade data, per a market calendar listing.

December 3: Nvidia's Jensen Huang is scheduled to appear at a U.S. Senate hearing, Yonhap reported.
