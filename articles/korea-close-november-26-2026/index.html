Korea Close | November 26, 2026

The Bank of Korea raised its policy rate to 3.25 percent and signaled more to come. Short-dated bond yields moved up with it. The KOSPI's rebound from a 6.16 percent drop on November 25 faded to a 0.29 percent gain by the close.

On Thursday, November 26, 2026, the KOSPI opened 1.01 percent higher and climbed as much as 2.56 percent before closing at 4,711.21. The central bank's third hike of the year came with sharply higher growth forecasts and a dot plot that leaned toward further tightening. Bonds sold off, led by the front of the curve, and the won barely moved.

The close

KTB 3-year 3.622 percent, up 3.9 basis points. KTB 10-year 3.790 percent, up 2.9 basis points. Both are from the 4:40 p.m. KST closing bond table published by Yonhap, and the 3-year matches the Bank of Korea's ECOS daily close.

USD/KRW 1,342.7 at the 15:30 Seoul close, 0.5 won stronger than the previous session's 1,343.2.

KOSPI 4,711.21, up 13.70 points, or 0.29 percent.

KOSDAQ 655.71, up 5.07 points, or 0.78 percent.

KRX cash market, KOSPI: institutions net bought about 750 billion won and individuals net sold about 1.04 trillion won, per Yonhap's closing reports. Foreigners were small net sellers of about 110 billion won, with outlet tallies differing slightly. None of these figures include NextTrade.

What changed

The Monetary Policy Board lifted the base rate by 25 basis points, from 3.00 percent to 3.25 percent, after hikes in July and August and a pause in October. Two members dissented in favor of a hold. The bank raised its growth forecast to 3.9 percent for 2026 from 3.3 percent, and to 4.1 percent for 2027 from 2.9 percent, citing semiconductor exports driven by AI demand.

The dot plot was the bigger story. Of 21 dots for the rate six months out, 7 sat at 3.75 percent, 8 at 3.50 percent and 6 at 3.25 percent. Yonhap put the median at 3.50 percent, up from 3.25 percent. Yonhap Infomax reported the 3-year yield 7.0 basis points higher at 10:50 a.m. KST before it pared the move. The 10-year rose less than the 3-year, and the 30-year slipped 0.9 basis point to 3.927 percent, according to Aju Press, citing the Korea Financial Investment Association.

In equities, the chip leaders that crashed on November 25 won back only a small part of their losses. SK hynix rose 2.23 percent to 1,053,000 won after trading as high as 1,098,000 won. Samsung Electronics added 0.35 percent to 171,300 won, well off its intraday high of 176,700 won. Foreigners net bought 314.1 billion won of SK hynix and net sold 97.1 billion won of Samsung Electronics, Yonhap reported.

Where the signal stops

Breadth was close to even, with 440 decliners against 425 gainers, according to Yonhap. Autos lagged. Hyundai Motor fell 3.9 percent, Kia 2.4 percent and Hyundai Mobis 5.7 percent, Seoul Economic Daily reported. Regular-session KOSPI trading value came to 12.28 trillion won, the lowest since December 24, 2025, according to Korea Exchange data cited by Yonhap. The KOSDAQ touched 673.57 early and finished near its session low of 654.80.

In currencies, the dollar dipped to 1,339.3 won at 11:09 a.m. KST, a minute before the governor's press conference, then drifted back. Yonhap pointed to month-end exporter dollar sales on one side and dollar demand tied to the Samsung Electronics dividend payment on November 27 on the other.

RYZM's reading: The hike was expected. The dots were the surprise. Dealers quoted by Yonhap Infomax said more of them landed at 3.75 percent than the market had looked for. The front end of the curve took that in, while the 30-year yield edged lower. The won shrugged off a policy rate gap with the United States that narrowed to 0.75 percentage point, per Yonhap. In stocks, the thin volume and the fade from a 2.56 percent gain suggest buyers are testing the chip selloff, not reversing it.

Global link

On November 25 in New York, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62 percent after a deeper intraday drop, Yonhap reported. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 0.65 percent, Nvidia 3.91 percent, Micron 4.67 percent and the SK hynix ADR 0.57 percent, Financial News reported. Yonhap tied the morning bounce in Seoul's chip leaders to that late recovery in U.S. tech.
