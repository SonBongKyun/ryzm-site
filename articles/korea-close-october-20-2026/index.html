Korea Close | October 20, 2026

Korea Close | October 20, 2026

The KOSPI fell 3.73% (-3.73%) to 5,552.74, erasing two days of gains and closing at its lowest level since April 7. The KOSDAQ dropped 3.33% (-3.33%) to 781.97. Both benchmarks sank more than 3% in a single session.

Rates moved first. France’s political and fiscal turmoil pushed its 10-year yield up 12 basis points to 4.9%. U.S. Treasury yields followed. The 10-year rose 6.69 basis points to 5.1397%. Houthi attacks on Saudi oil facilities pushed November WTI futures up 2.22% to 103.66 dollars. Higher funding costs hit AI infrastructure names, which borrow to build. CoreWeave gave up an intraday gain of nearly 8% to close down 0.4%. The Philadelphia semiconductor index faded after jumping 3.7%.

The won kept doing the opposite. USD/KRW closed the Seoul spot day session at 1,328.1, down 2.2 won on the day (-2.2 won), another two-year low. Strong September exports and corporate dollar selling have overwhelmed the usual drivers. The dollar index held above 100, yet the won strengthened.

Equities fell in the gap between those two forces. Foreigners net sold roughly 530 billion won of KOSPI shares and institutions about 1.0 trillion won, per News1’s tally. Retail investors bought a net 898 billion won. Samsung Electronics lost 2.98% to 211,500 won. SK hynix fell 4.23% to 1,223,000 won. Defense names sold off after earnings disappointment. Hyundai Rotem dropped 13.70%.

The day’s verdict came from the presidential office. On October 19, President Lee Jae-myung called Korean stocks severely undervalued, pointed to controlling-shareholder governance and market transparency, and promised additional measures after a comprehensive report.

One more shock to sentiment arrived at listing. Elice Group, reportedly this year’s largest AI IPO, fell as much as 32% on its KOSDAQ debut.

The Bank of Korea meets on October 22. The test is whether domestic rates policy can hold the line while global yields keep tightening.
