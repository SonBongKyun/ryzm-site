Korea’s Early-November Exports More Than Double as Chips Near Half of All Shipments | November 23, 2026

South Korea just printed the strongest 20-day export number in its history, on the same morning its stock market triggered a program-selling curb for the second session in a row. The two facts point in opposite directions, and that tension is the story.

Preliminary customs data released on Monday, November 23, show exports of $77.4 billion for November 1 to 20, up 101.5% from $38.4 billion a year earlier. The Korea Customs Service said it was the highest figure ever recorded for the first 20 days of any month. The previous record was set only two months ago, at $71.6 billion in the first 20 days of September 2026.

THE NUMBERS

  • Exports: $77.40 billion, up 101.5%, an increase of $38.98 billion
  • Imports: $50.29 billion, up 39.4%
  • Trade balance: a surplus of $27.11 billion, against $2.35 billion a year earlier
  • Daily average exports: $4.84 billion, up 95.2%, adjusting for 16.0 working days this year versus 15.5 last year
  • Year to date (January 1 to November 20): exports of $1,007.6 billion, up 63.2%, with a cumulative surplus of $322.1 billion

The year-to-date figure is the headline the agency chose. On its preliminary customs-clearance basis, Korea’s cumulative exports have now passed $1 trillion for the first time.

The working-day adjustment matters for reading the jump honestly. Half a day more of trading flatters a raw 20-day comparison a little. But the daily average still nearly doubled, so the calendar explains very little of this.

ONE PRODUCT IS DOING MOST OF THE WORK

Semiconductor exports reached $38.47 billion, up 294.4%, which means they nearly quadrupled. That is also a record for the period. Chips now account for 49.7% of everything Korea shipped in the first 20 days of the month, up 24.3 percentage points from a year earlier.

Strip chips out and the picture is still strong but far less dramatic. Using the agency’s own figures, RYZM estimates non-chip exports at about $38.9 billion, up roughly 36% from about $28.7 billion a year ago. That is healthy growth. It is not a doubling.

Other notable lines:

  • Petroleum products: $4.13 billion, up 91.9%, a category that tends to move with crude prices
  • Computer peripherals: $3.67 billion, up 330.6%
  • Steel products: $2.86 billion, up 27.0%
  • Ships: $2.14 billion, up 85.1%
  • Passenger cars: $3.59 billion, down 5.6%, one of the few major categories that shrank

By destination, exports to China rose 145.6% to $20.05 billion. Exports to the United States rose 142.7% to $16.44 billion. Vietnam was up 85.8%, Taiwan up 149.7%, and Singapore up 229.6%. China, the US and Vietnam together took 55.5% of the total. Malaysia, another electronics manufacturing hub, was up 174.7%.

The import side tells its own story. Chip imports doubled, up 103.4% to $9.31 billion. Crude oil imports rose 83.4% and gas imports rose 93.9%. Energy imports as a group were up 78.5%. Korea is paying much more for fuel, yet the surplus still widened more than tenfold.

WHY THE MARKET IS NOT CELEBRATING

At 11:33 a.m. KST on Monday, the Korea Exchange suspended program sell orders on the main board for five minutes after KOSPI 200 futures fell 5.08% to 714.50, Yonhap reported. It was the second straight session with a sell-side sidecar on the KOSPI, after Friday’s 5.21% drop in the index. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, the two companies behind most of those chip exports, were each down more than 5% at the time.

The disconnect is not a mystery. Trade data describe shipments that have already happened, some of them under the long-term supply contracts the finance minister referred to on Monday. Equity markets trade on what investors think AI spending will look like in 2027 and beyond. Friday’s sharp tech sell-off in New York, driven by doubts about big tech’s AI infrastructure outlays, hit exactly the names that dominate both Korea’s export mix and the KOSPI’s weighting.

That concentration is the risk worth naming. When half of a country’s exports come from one product, the trade numbers and the stock market become two readings of the same bet. Strong shipments today do not settle the question of whether demand holds up next year.

THE GOVERNMENT’S READ

Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il, speaking to reporters in Sejong on Monday, said the government expects the global chip cycle to stay firm next year, citing a synthesis of major forecasters. He said Samsung and SK hynix have secured substantial volumes through long-term supply contracts and that their memory inventories have kept falling, according to Yonhap.

Lee said real GDP growth in the high 3% range looks possible this year on current trends, adding that 4% is “within the range” arithmetically. On markets, he said the government has a contingency plan beyond the stock stabilization fund that authorities agreed on Sunday to prepare. He stopped short of saying the fund would be activated, and said a decision would follow a close review of conditions.

On the currency, Lee said that as current account surpluses build up, the exchange rate should follow fundamentals. The won was quoted at 1,346.5 per dollar at 11:20 a.m. KST, roughly flat on the day, according to Yonhap.

WHAT TO WATCH

  • Full-month November trade data, expected on December 1, which will show whether the 20-day pace held through month-end
  • The Bank of Korea’s rate decision this week. Government bond yields eased Monday morning, with the 3-year at 3.621%, down 2.1 basis points, Yonhap reported
  • Any formal decision on the stabilization fund, as distinct from readiness to use it

The customs agency itself flags the limits of this release. Short-period figures can be distorted by differences in working days, and some numbers may be revised until annual statistics are finalized in February 2027.
