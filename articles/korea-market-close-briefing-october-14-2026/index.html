Korea Market Close Briefing | October 14, 2026

Korea Close | October 14, 2026

The KOSPI closed at 6,008.81, down 26.54 points (-0.44%), for its sixth straight decline. The KOSDAQ fell to 823.88 (-1.57%).

USD/KRW ended the Seoul day session at 1,338.3 (-6.8 won) on the 3:30 p.m. spot close.

Rates set the morning and lost the day. The U.S. 10-year eased to 5.21%, down 3 bp, as bond trading resumed after the Columbus Day holiday and dovish Fed comments cooled rate-hike fears. The KOSPI opened 0.44% higher at 6,062.08 and reached 2.02% by mid-morning on oversold buying, with foreigners briefly net buyers. Samsung Electronics traded 3.34% higher and SK hynix 2.49% higher in the morning session.

The bounce did not hold. Foreigners turned sellers into the close and finished with KRW 1.36 trillion of net KOSPI selling, the sixth consecutive selling day (KIS 15:45 preliminary). Institutions bought KRW 969.2 billion, led by financial investment accounts, but the afternoon slide carried the index through 6,000 intraday before a thin hold into the close.

The won refused to follow the equity script. Foreign stock selling usually bids the dollar higher, but exporter dollar supply, heavy from semiconductor exporters converting overseas revenue, capped the rise all session. The won closed 6.8 won stronger instead.

Rotation, not breadth. SK hynix fell 1.66%, a sixth straight decline, as foreigners made it their top sale at KRW 752.1 billion. Samsung SDI rose 5.54% on energy-storage expectations, and the board rewarded everything semiconductors did not touch. Shipping rose 4.9%, with Pan Ocean up 10.53%, on the Saudi-Houthi escalation. Pipe-fitting and valve names gained 6.31% after Trump name-checked Alaska LNG. Finance dropped 2.06% as Shinhan weakened on the Lotte Sonbo acquisition.

What reaches Thursday: the memory repricing is unresolved. Bloomberg called CXMT’s 4F2 DRAM breakthrough claim unsubstantiated, but U.S. memory names fell a second straight night and foreigners kept selling Seoul’s memory beta. The September CPI report, delayed by the shutdown, now lands October 24, days before the Fed’s October 28-29 meeting. The reference for Thursday is clear: the failed 2% morning bounce and the 6,000 line the index barely kept.
