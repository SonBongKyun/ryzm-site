Korea Market Close Briefing | October 15, 2026

Korea Close | October 15, 2026

The KOSPI closed at 5,554.22, down 454.59 points (-7.57%), for a seventh straight decline. The KOSDAQ fell to 760.82 (-7.65%). Both markets halted trading for 20 minutes under circuit breakers, the first simultaneous breakers since late July.

USD/KRW ended the Seoul day session at 1,344.6 (+6.3 won) on the 3:30 p.m. spot close.

Rates set the morning and kept the day. September CPI rose 3.7% from a year earlier, above the 3.6% consensus, and core inflation hit 2.6%, topping the 2.5% forecast. The U.S. 10-year spiked above 5.32% right after the release before easing to 5.189%, down 3 bp on the day. Markets now price roughly a one-in-three chance of a Fed hike on October 28, up from about 22% on Tuesday. The KOSPI opened through 6,000 and never looked back, sliding more than 8% intraday and briefly through 5,500 before the breakers stopped the slide.

Foreigners sold KRW 3.62 trillion of KOSPI shares, while institutions bought KRW 1.69 trillion and individuals KRW 1.29 trillion. On the KOSDAQ, foreigners were small net buyers at KRW 215.9 billion as institutions and individuals sold.

The AI credit trade turned on Seoul’s memory names. CoreWeave bond yields reached 15% and Gartner warned of $3.5 trillion in impaired AI assets. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 4.3% for a sixth straight session. The counter-evidence sat in plain view: TSMC posted a 69% profit jump to a record and ASML reaffirmed strong AI equipment demand. The market heard none of it. Samsung Electronics fell 9.48% to KRW 210,000 and SK hynix dropped 11.06% to KRW 1,262,000.

Defensive names rose: Samsung Biologics 5.50%, Yuhan 5.09%, KT&G 3.96%. S1 jumped 13.89% on a KRW 450 billion buyback plan. Financial authorities convened an emergency review after the close.

What reaches Friday: the valuation argument is forming. Samsung Securities named 5,000 as support, and Daishin’s Lee Kyung-min noted the 12-month forward price-earnings ratio below 4.5, with the 40.4% drawdown from the June 19 peak near the 43.7% median of the past seven crisis phases. The question is whether Friday’s Warsh speech at the IMF meeting gives the market a reason to stop selling growth first.

Sources: KRX via Yonhap News TV and Seoul Economic Daily; Newsis for USD/KRW; WSJ and BLS for CPI; CME FedWatch via WSJ; Reuters for CoreWeave; TSMC and ASML for earnings.
