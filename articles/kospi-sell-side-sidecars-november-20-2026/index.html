Korea triggers sell-side sidecars as the KOSPI falls more than 5% | November 20, 2026

South Korea’s stock market was still trading lower in early afternoon on Friday, November 20, 2026, after the Korea Exchange activated sell-side sidecars on both the main board and the KOSDAQ. As of 1:33 p.m. KST, the KOSPI stood at 4,873.65, down 264.24 points, or 5.14 percent, from Thursday’s close of 5,137.89. The KOSDAQ was at 652.46, down 32.35 points, or 4.72 percent. Those figures are timed prints from a session that had not yet closed.

A sidecar is a brief pause on program selling, not a halt in all trading. The tool is meant to slow a burst of index-linked sell orders when futures move far enough, and long enough, to meet the exchange’s tests.

What triggered the curbs

The first curb came on the KOSPI at 11:54:05 a.m. KST. A Korea Exchange market notice, carried by Sedaily Signal, showed the most actively traded KOSPI 200 futures contract, based on the prior session’s volume, at 755.84, down 40.16 points, or 5.04 percent, from a reference price of 796.00. Under the exchange’s cash-market rules, a decline of 5 percent or more that lasts at least one minute suspends the effect of program sell orders for five minutes.

A second curb followed on the KOSDAQ at about 1:28 p.m. KST, Yonhap reported, citing the exchange. At the trigger, KOSDAQ 150 futures were at 1,072.80, down 69.00 points, or 6.04 percent. The KOSDAQ 150 cash index was at 1,070.37, down 63.79 points, or 5.62 percent. KOSDAQ rules require both a futures drop of 6 percent or more and a KOSDAQ 150 decline of 3 percent or more from the previous close, each lasting one minute, before program selling is paused for five minutes. Yonhap said it was the first day both boards had triggered sell-side sidecars since November 6, 10 trading sessions earlier.

The damage was already visible at the open. The KOSPI began the day at 4,928.51, down 209.38 points, or 4.08 percent. The KOSDAQ opened at 660.98, down 23.83 points, or 3.48 percent.

The global backdrop

The Seoul decline followed a weak close on Wall Street on Thursday, November 19. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1,101.11 points, or 2.19 percent, to 49,097.06. The S&P 500 fell 147.80 points, or 2.02 percent, to 7,175.83. The Nasdaq Composite fell 550.69 points, or 2.25 percent, to 23,879.20.

Anthropic, which had priced its initial public offering at $83.00 a share on November 18, began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19 under the symbol ANT. Yahoo Finance recorded an open of $111.50, a high of $115.10, a low of $92.72, and a close of $97.17, up $14.17, or 17.07 percent, from the offering price. The first-day close was below the opening print. That debut did not lift the wider AI-linked complex. Yonhap’s English market reports said Seoul opened lower as investors continued to ask whether heavy spending on AI infrastructure will produce sustainable returns.

In Tokyo, the Nikkei 225 finished the morning session at 56,775.04 and started the afternoon at 56,812.05, down 2,632.16 points on the day, according to Zaikei. The morning level was 4.49 percent below Thursday’s close. Yonhap also reported Taiwan’s TAIEX at 36,089.60 around midday, down 6.08 percent from Thursday’s official close of 38,426.89, published by the Taiwan Stock Exchange. The Friday Taiwan figure is an intraday reading, not a settlement price.

Korea’s heavyweights

Chipmakers led the move. At 1:33 p.m. KST, Yonhap said Samsung Electronics and SK hynix were each down about 6.7 percent. A little earlier, at 1:16 p.m., Aju Press put Samsung at 184,600 won, down 6.25 percent, and SK hynix at 1,109,000 won, down 6.26 percent, from Thursday’s closes of 196,900 won and 1,183,000 won.

Investor-flow figures from mid-morning point to selling by professional accounts and buying by individuals, though those totals can change before the close. As of 11:20 a.m. KST, Yonhap reported that foreign investors were net sellers of 760.54 billion won of KOSPI shares and institutions were net sellers of 443.61 billion won, while individuals were net buyers of 1.01 trillion won. The won was quoted at 1,338.30 per dollar at the same time, 4.2 won weaker than the previous session’s close, according to that report.

What to watch

Korean regular trading is scheduled to end at 3:30 p.m. KST. The index levels, share prices, and buying and selling totals in this note are timed observations from a session that was still open when the sources were checked in early afternoon in Seoul. A sidecar expires after five minutes and does not, by itself, set the day’s close. The next hard facts are the official settlement prices, the final foreign and institutional flow figures, and the afternoon prints in Tokyo.

This note does not forecast the close or recommend any trade.
