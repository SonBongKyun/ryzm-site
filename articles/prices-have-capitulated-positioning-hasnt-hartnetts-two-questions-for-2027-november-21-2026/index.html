Prices Have Capitulated, Positioning Hasn’t: Hartnett’s Two Questions for 2027 | November 21, 2026

Source note: Based on Bank of America's Flow Show (Michael Hartnett, November 19, 2026) and BofA's November Global Fund Manager Survey, as relayed in secondary coverage. Figures are BofA's. This is a summary of BofA's framework, not investment advice or a market call.

Bank of America strategist Michael Hartnett sums up the current selloff in one line in his latest Flow Show: prices have capitulated, but positioning has not. He frames 2027 around two questions. When do rate hikes end? And do they end before a recession arrives?

Flows: money kept coming in

In a week of sharp equity declines, equity funds took in $66.6 billion. The four-week total hit a record $310 billion. US growth funds drew $9.1 billion, the most since December 2024. Tech funds logged an eighth straight week of inflows.

BofA's Bull & Bear Indicator slipped from 7.4 to 7.1, but its parts point in different directions. Equity inflows sit at the 99th percentile and fund manager positioning at the 100th. The indicator's global equity breadth reading, meanwhile, has dropped to 18%. The November fund manager survey shows cash at 3.6%. Only 3% of respondents expect a hard landing. 50% expect a soft landing and 42% expect no landing. BofA private clients hold 65.7% in equities, 17.7% in bonds and 9.4% in cash.

Rates and funding: the bond cushion isn't working

The US 30-year Treasury is on track for a sixth straight annual price decline, the longest run since 1963 to 1969. The Nasdaq fell 15%, banks 16% and oil 22%. Over the same stretch, the 30-year yield came down only 25 basis points from its recent high. Bond funds saw a $7.4 billion outflow, the first since April 2025. Emerging-market debt funds lost $7.5 billion, the most since April 2020. Hartnett's main concern is that debt levels are now high enough for yields to rise, not fall, in the next recession.

Two paths for the hiking cycle

BofA's report counts 71 rate hikes between June 2026 and May 2027. In the first path, a Fed that won't let markets break ends the cycle early once stress hits credit and equities. BofA's reference levels for that stress are investment-grade CDX above 80bp and the S&P 500 below 6,845. In the second path, a Fed focused on inflation after the midterms keeps hiking. Here the earliest meetings where markets could price an end are the March 18 and April 28 FOMC meetings.

Markers Hartnett is watching

  • A peak in the dollar, which rallied toward 104 after the September hike.
  • Higher lows in assets that peaked around Fed events, such as Bitcoin, gold and the KOSPI.
  • The US 10-year yield below 4.5% and WTI below $70.
  • Weekly jobless claims above 250,000 and investment-grade spreads above 100bp, which he treats as recession markers.
  • BofA's Global Breadth Rule, which triggers when 88% of indices trade below both their 50-day and 200-day moving averages. It currently reads 68%.

Korea bridge

For Korea, this framework runs through two variables: the dollar and US long-term yields. The KOSPI is one of the assets BofA ties to Fed-event peaks. That makes the dollar and the long end of the Treasury curve the main things to watch for USD/KRW and for foreign flows into Korean stocks and KTBs.

Hartnett's historical reference is 2000 and 2001. The Nasdaq staged 12 rallies of 10% or more on the way to its low, and the dollar stayed strong in both years.
