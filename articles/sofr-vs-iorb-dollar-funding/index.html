SOFR vs IORB: Reading Dollar Funding Pressure

RYZM REFERENCE / FUNDING & LIQUIDITY

The level of SOFR tells you what overnight Treasury financing costs. Its relationship to IORB helps frame why. The spread is useful, but calling every positive reading a dollar shortage turns a diagnostic into an alarm that never switches off.

In this guide: Two rates · Calculation · Date alignment · Context · Desk framework · Sources

SOFR and IORB measure different things

SOFR, the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, measures the cost of borrowing cash overnight against Treasury collateral. The New York Fed calculates it as a transaction-volume-weighted median across specified repo markets. It is not a quote for every borrower and it is not an unsecured bank credit rate. Source: New York Fed SOFR definition.

IORB is the interest rate the Federal Reserve pays on reserve balances held by eligible institutions. It is an administered policy rate, determined by the Board, rather than a median of market transactions. The two rates have different counterparties and economic roles. Source: Federal Reserve IORB documentation.

How to calculate SOFR minus IORB

Spread in basis points = (SOFR in percent minus IORB in percent) × 100. If inputs are decimals such as 0.04 rather than percentages such as 4.00, multiply their difference by 10,000 instead.

Hypothetical example: SOFR of 4.02% and IORB of 4.00% produce a spread of +2 basis points. After a policy-rate change, readings of 3.77% and 3.75% also produce +2 basis points. SOFR fell 25 basis points, but this relative funding measure did not change.

The comparison strips out a common level shift in that example. It does not strip out every difference between the markets. A +2 basis point spread is neither +2% nor a measurement of the total dollars available to the financial system.

Match effective dates, not the time you opened the page

The daily SOFR is published one business day after its value date. The Federal Reserve’s IORB table can display the rate effective on the next business day. Pairing the two most recently displayed values without checking their effective dates can create a false spread, especially around policy changes and holidays. Source: New York Fed publication methodology. Source: Federal Reserve update convention.

Our data record should therefore contain the observation date, publication date, units, source URL and any revision flag. A Seoul timestamp records when the analysis was prepared; it must not silently replace the US market observation date.

A positive spread needs context

Federal Reserve research treats repo rates relative to IORB as one of several indicators of reserve conditions. Its framework also considers effective federal funds pricing, the responsiveness of rates to changes in reserves and money-market volatility. Competition for reserves and the costs of deploying bank balance sheets both enter the analysis. Source: Federal Reserve market-indicator framework, January 2025.

Quarter-end is a separate test case. In a June 2025 note, Federal Reserve researchers describe how some dealers reduce intermediation around reporting dates, increasing repo intermediation spreads and rates. A move that reverses after a reporting date deserves a different explanation from persistent pressure on ordinary days. That does not make calendar effects irrelevant; their changing size can also be informative. Source: Federal Reserve quarter-end analysis.

RYZM desk framework: persistence, breadth and quantities

Our interpretation framework: examine whether the spread remains elevated outside settlement or reporting dates, whether adjacent markets move with it, and what happens to volumes. One unusually high observation warrants checking the data before declaring a system-wide condition.

The New York Fed publishes transaction volumes and percentile rates alongside its Treasury repo reference rates. A volume-weighted median can conceal a more expensive tail of transactions, so the distribution is a useful companion to the headline. Compare equivalent percentiles over the same dates rather than treating one tail print as the market’s typical rate. Source: New York Fed calculation methodology.

A broad reserve-scarcity explanation is weaker when the spread normalises immediately, nearby indicators remain stable, or the movement can be explained by a shift in the transactions entering the benchmark. It becomes more credible when multiple indicators show persistent pressure. These are tests to investigate, not fixed trading thresholds.

What this spread cannot establish

It cannot identify a bank in trouble. SOFR summarises secured transactions; it is not a bank solvency test. It cannot, on its own, diagnose Korean dollar funding. For that question, specify the borrower, currency, tenor and hedge instrument rather than extending a US overnight statistic to every offshore balance sheet.

The practical rule is to keep the claim no larger than the evidence: which funding market became more expensive, relative to what benchmark, and for how long?
