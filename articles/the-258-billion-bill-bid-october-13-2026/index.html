The $258 Billion Bill Bid | October 13, 2026

Tether’s first-quarter attestation put a number on a structural shift the front end of the Treasury market has already absorbed. The issuer of USDT held $141 billion of direct and indirect U.S. Treasury exposure at the end of March, BDO reported, which Tether says makes it the 17th-largest holder of U.S. government debt in the world. A crypto company’s reserve desk now sits in the same ranking as sovereign wealth funds.

The mechanism is mechanical rather than ideological. Every USDT and USDC is backed close to one for one by reserves parked in T-bills, overnight repo, and cash. Circle’s dashboard showed about $73.2 billion of reserves against $73 billion of circulating USDC on October 8, most of it in a BlackRock-managed government money-market fund limited to short Treasuries, cash, and overnight Treasury repo. Tether’s second-quarter attestation, as of June 30, showed $184.6 billion of USDT issued against $114.96 billion of directly held T-bills, with the balance in repo, gold, and bitcoin.

Put the two together and the stablecoin system is roughly $258 billion of tokenized dollar demand, almost all of it warehouse space in short-term U.S. government paper. Total stablecoin supply reached $300.9 billion on October 1, according to Token Terminal and the RWA Foundation. That is a new holder class at the front end, sitting next to money market funds, and it behaves like no other buyer in the market.

Money funds buy bills because the yield compensates them. Stablecoin issuers buy bills because someone wanted a dollar that settles on a blockchain. The marginal bid is driven by crypto leverage appetite, trading demand, and offshore dollar preference, not by anyone’s rate view. When stablecoin supply expands, mechanical bill buying arrives with it, landing in the market just as October’s $225 billion bill wave needs buyers. When redemptions come, that bid leaves on the same schedule.

The read: stablecoin supply growth is a funding-market variable now, not a crypto variable. It is pro-cyclical marginal demand for T-bills, arriving when risk appetite is strong and departing when it is not. Tether’s third-quarter attestation is not out yet, so the Treasury figures above are June 30 marks. Watch the weekly mint-and-burn flows the way you watch money-fund flows. The Treasury market’s newest marginal buyer does not attend auctions and does not read the dot plot. It converts dollar demand into bill demand, one token at a time.

Sources and data scope

Source review: 14 October 2026 (KST). These references support the specific observations below; publication time is not a market-data timestamp.

The reserve breakdown also includes secured loans, public equities and other investments, beyond the repo, gold and bitcoin named above.

Circle’s USDC disclosure shows $73.0 billion in circulation and $73.2 billion in reserves dated 8 October 2026. This is a changing issuer page; retain the observation date when comparing it with quarterly reports.

Comparison limit: the roughly $258 billion figure combines token amounts from different dates. It is neither a same-date total nor a measurement of Treasury holdings. The opening $141 billion figure is a March observation, not a June observation. The $300.9 billion industry total and $225 billion October bill-supply figure have not been independently verified in this source review. The funding-market implications remain RYZM’s interpretation.
