The Long End’s New Buyers | October 15, 2026

THE LONG END’S NEW BUYERS

On September 29, Japan sold 300 billion yen of 40-year bonds. The auction drew 3.1 times the amount on offer, the strongest bid-to-cover since July 2020. Days later the 30-year drew 3.88 times at an average yield of 4.109%. This reads like demand. Look closer: the price is doing the buying.

Japan’s long end is being repriced because the old buyer is leaving and the traditional one cannot move. The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to 1.25% on September 18, the highest in 31 years, and its bond buying is on track for about 2.4 to 2.6 trillion yen a month under the QT plan, heading toward 2.1 trillion by the first quarter of 2027. Oxford Economics puts the central bank’s share of the JGB market at roughly 47%, and falling.

The life insurers that used to buy duration without question are pinned by valuation losses. Nippon Life reported 4.164 trillion yen in paper losses at the end of June, the largest on record, and says it is only considering a return to net buying from fiscal 2027, led by replacement purchases. Four of ten major insurers told Reuters they expect to increase yen bond holdings this fiscal year. The rest do not.

Meanwhile the Ministry of Finance is shrinking super-long supply itself. The fiscal 2026 plan cuts 20-, 30- and 40-year issuance by 100 billion yen a month, out of 180.7 trillion yen in total issuance. Supply is contracting into the central bank’s vacuum.

So who is buying? A different crowd. Japanese bond ETFs have taken in a record $1.5 billion so far in 2026, about triple last year’s, mostly from European real money. And domestic money is coming home. Japanese institutions sold a net 1.9 trillion yen of medium-term JGBs from April to August, but Deutsche Bank notes that once hedging costs are counted, Treasuries are now less attractive than JGBs for Japanese buyers. Insurer hedging ratios on overseas securities fell to 47.8% in September, the largest drop since 2011.

The auctions are strong because 4% yields are doing the buying, not because the old buyer base came back. The long end works as a market again. That is the real change.

Unanswered: nobody knows how much insurers are actually buying month to month. Without that, the auction strength is real but fragile. The repricing is genuine. The buyer base is not yet rebuilt.
