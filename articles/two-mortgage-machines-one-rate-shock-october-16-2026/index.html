Two Mortgage Machines, One Rate Shock | October 16, 2026

TWO MORTGAGE MACHINES, ONE RATE SHOCK

The US 30-year mortgage rate reached 7.50% last week, the highest since November 2023, MBA data show. Purchase applications fell 4% on the week and ran 16% below a year ago. The ARM share of applications hit 10.5%, its highest in a year, as borrowers reached for lower payments.

In Korea, September COFIX printed 3.43%, up 25bp in a month. That is the biggest jump since November 2022 and the highest since June 2024. Banks begin passing it through today. NH Nonghyup’s variable mortgage now tops out at 6.48%, and three of the five major banks charge above 6% at the top end.

Same global rate shock. Two different machines delivering it to households.

The US machine runs on the stock, not the flow. About half of outstanding US mortgages still carry rates of 4% or lower and nearly 80% sit below 6%, Fed Governor Barr noted last month, citing Fed staff calculations on the National Mortgage Database. The American mortgage stock is overwhelmingly fixed-rate. A 7.50% quote does not reprice the fixed-rate stock. It prices the next buyer, and the next buyer is walking away or taking an ARM. Against a 10-year Treasury near 5.1%, the mortgage rate embeds a spread of about 240bp, the market’s charge for prepayment risk and MBS liquidity, moving on its own. Lock-in does the rest. Owners holding 4% loans have little reason to sell, so supply thins while demand cools. The pain shows up in turnover, not in monthly payments.

Korea’s machine runs the other way. COFIX is the weighted funding cost of eight banks, and variable-rate mortgages reset off it on six-month or one-year cycles. New loans price off the 3.43% new-amount print. Existing borrowers reset off the slower balance-based measures as their reset dates arrive, and the balance COFIX already rose to 3.15%. A 25bp COFIX jump lands in monthly payments within months. Variable-rate loans are 38.3% of new mortgages and rising, and the BOK’s household debt data put a third of borrowers in their 40s at debt-service ratios of 40% or more.

The read: in the US the mortgage rate is a flow variable, so watch purchase applications and the ARM share, not the quote. In Korea it is a stock variable, so watch the COFIX reset calendar and how fast the balance-based print follows. With the BOK at 3.00% after August’s back-to-back hikes and the market pricing a higher terminal rate, the resets keep coming.
