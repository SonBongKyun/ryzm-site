US Close | November 25, 2026

The Nasdaq Composite was down 4.6 percent at its low and finished with a 0.62 percent loss as buyers came back before Thanksgiving. Treasuries rallied even though core inflation ran above forecast.

By midmorning on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, it looked like a rout: AI stocks sliding again, a hot core PCE print, and new U.S. tariff proposals aimed at 14 economies, Korea among them. By the 4 p.m. ET close, the Dow was up 164.25 points and the S&P 500 was down a quarter of a percent. U.S. stock and bond markets are closed November 26 for Thanksgiving.

The close

Treasury 2-year 4.61 percent, down 2 basis points. Treasury 10-year 4.85 percent, down 6 basis points. Both are from Treasury's daily par yield curve. A late-session CNBC market quote showed a smaller 10-year drop, about 3 basis points to 4.866 percent, so the size of the 10-year move is not independently verified.

Dollar index about 103.6, down roughly 0.2 percent in late-session wraps. Not independently verified at the close. USD/KRW last closed at 1,343.2 at 3:30 p.m. Seoul time on November 25, before New York opened.

Brent (January) settled at $94.61 a barrel, up $3.21, or 3.51 percent. WTI (January) settled at $83.68, up $1.63, or 1.99 percent.

S&P 500 7,182.78, down 18.31 points, or 0.25 percent.

Nasdaq Composite 23,585.80, down 147.73 points, or 0.62 percent.

Dow Jones Industrial Average 50,182.95, up 164.25 points, or 0.33 percent.

PHLX Semiconductor Index 8,774.86, down 0.65 percent.

VIX 28.29, up 1.93 points, or 7.32 percent, after touching 34.95 during the session.

Index levels are official closing values. Reuters' preliminary prints, such as 7,183.08 for the S&P 500, are not used.

What changed

The Bureau of Economic Analysis said the PCE price index rose 0.3 percent in October and 3.8 percent from a year earlier, up from 3.7 percent. Core PCE rose 3.3 percent against a 3.2 percent consensus. Third-quarter GDP was revised to a 4.3 percent annual pace. CNBC said the data reinforced expectations for Fed hikes in December and January.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer also determined that the manufacturing policies of 14 economies, including China, the European Union, Japan, Taiwan and Korea, were actionable under Section 301 of the Trade Act, and proposed tariffs.

The AI trade kept bleeding. Nvidia fell 3.91 percent to $149.30. S&P Global put Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch Negative, citing management and governance risk. Micron lost 4.53 percent and Oracle 5.13 percent. Ambarella dropped 19.77 percent after a quarter that missed estimates and a light forecast. The company said some customers canceled or delayed orders because of soaring memory prices.

At the lows, the S&P 500 was down 3.2 percent and the Dow had lost more than 1,100 points. CNBC tied part of the comeback to thin holiday trading and a Michael Burry post saying he was "temporarily almost entirely long."

Where the signal stops

The bounce ran through the rest of the market. The Russell 2000 rose 0.7 percent to 2,704.06. Materials, energy and real estate gained, and technology led the sector decliners. Volume was below average, Reuters said. Reuters' 2.08 to 1 NYSE advance ratio was taken before the close, so we leave final breadth out.

Oil moved on separate news. Yemen's Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, and PVM's Tamas Varga said the claim set off the rally, with short covering ahead of the holiday adding to it.

RYZM's reading: The close flatters the session. Yields fell on a hot core print. The VIX ended higher with the S&P 500 near flat. The rebound came on light pre-holiday volume. So the reversal looks more like dip buying than a verdict on AI.

The Korea link

The USTR notice on Korea proposes tariffs of up to 15 percent on all Korean goods, net of MFN rates and existing Section 301 tariffs. Semiconductor articles and passenger vehicles already covered by Section 232 tariffs would be exempt. Yonhap said the proposal would add at most 2.5 percentage points on goods already paying the 12.5 percent forced-labor tariff. The notice keeps a fallback of up to 25 percent if USTR finds consultations with Seoul unsatisfactory. Written comments are due January 5, 2027, and hearings start January 25, 2027.

After the bell

November 26: U.S. stock and bond markets closed for Thanksgiving.

November 27: stocks close at 1 p.m. ET and bonds at 2 p.m. ET.

November 29: OPEC+ ministers meet on 2027 output policy.

December 1 and December 3: Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is scheduled to appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee and the Senate Banking Committee.

December 8 to 9: Federal Open Market Committee meeting.
