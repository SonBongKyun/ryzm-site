US Close | November 27, 2026

Chip stocks led a broad rebound in the shortened Black Friday session, with the PHLX Semiconductor Index up 4.69 percent. Treasuries sold off as the haven bid faded, and the S&P 500 logged its best week since April 2025.

Wall Street came back from Thanksgiving and bought what it had spent the month selling. On Friday, November 27, 2026, in a session that ended at 1 p.m. ET for stocks and 2 p.m. ET for bonds, every S&P 500 sector rose and Micron gained 7.6 percent. Nvidia, the stock at the center of November's turmoil, managed only 0.8 percent.

The close

Treasury 2-year 4.69 percent, up 8 basis points. Treasury 10-year 4.94 percent, up 9 basis points. Both are from Treasury's daily par yield curve, compared with November 25 because the bond market was closed November 26. Market quotes near the 2 p.m. ET bond close had the 10-year at about 4.93 percent.

Dollar index about 104.0, up roughly 0.4 percent in late-session data. Not independently verified at the close. USD/KRW closed at 1,337.9 at 3:30 p.m. Seoul time on November 27, down 4.8 won, before New York opened.

Brent (January) settled at $95.44 a barrel, down $1.13, or 1.17 percent. WTI (January) settled at $83.08, down 60 cents, or 0.72 percent, against its November 25 settlement, since there was no Thanksgiving settlement.

S&P 500 7,297.49, up 114.71 points, or 1.60 percent.

Nasdaq Composite 24,129.50, up 543.70 points, or 2.31 percent.

Dow Jones Industrial Average 50,774.94, up 591.99 points, or 1.18 percent.

PHLX Semiconductor Index 9,186.71, up 4.69 percent.

Russell 2000 2,750.30, up 1.71 percent.

VIX 23.81, down 2.99 points, or 11.16 percent, against the 26.80 value Cboe published for November 26.

Index levels are official closing values from the 1 p.m. ET stock close.

What changed

The buying went straight to the names that had been hit hardest. Micron rose 7.6 percent and Marvell 7.3 percent, Reuters said, and Sandisk jumped almost 16 percent. Anthropic gained 10.98 percent to $91.88, back above its $83 IPO price after a brief slip below it. Nvidia lagged. It ended up 0.8 percent after trading lower for most of the session.

It was a volatile week. It opened on November 23 with Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO, which lifted the stock 31 percent that day, CNBC said. Over the five sessions the S&P 500 gained 5.6 percent, its best week since April 2025. The Nasdaq rose 6.68 percent and the Dow 5.46 percent, the Dow's best week since October 2022.

Where the signal stops

Participation was wide. NYSE advancers beat decliners 3.25 to 1, and on the Nasdaq 3,677 stocks rose while 1,135 fell, Reuters said. All 11 S&P 500 sectors gained, led by consumer discretionary at 2.72 percent, according to Yonhap Infomax.

But this was a holiday half day, with 10.97 billion shares traded across U.S. exchanges. Black Friday shopping itself did not move the market. Adobe's tally of actual spending was not out at publication. Its September forecast called for $12.9 billion in Black Friday online spending, up 9.2 percent.

Bonds pointed the other way. Yields rose across maturities as stocks rallied, Bloomberg said, and Reuters said traders were almost fully pricing a Federal Reserve rate hike in December. Reuters also said the S&P 500 and Nasdaq remained on track for their worst November since 2008.

RYZM's reading: A short, thin session brought heavy chip buying, rising yields and a VIX still above 20. That looks more like bargain hunting than a settled verdict on AI returns. The bond market did not join the relief, and that is the harder signal to dismiss.

The Korea link

The link runs through memory chips. Micron rose 7.6 percent and SK hynix's U.S.-listed ADR gained more than 7 percent, according to Yonhap Infomax and YTN. Seoul moved first. On November 27 the KOSPI rose 4.34 percent to 4,915.68 and the KOSDAQ 8.13 percent to 708.99, with foreigners net buying 1.12 trillion won of KOSPI shares, Yonhap said. New York confirmed that move rather than starting it. The won's 1,337.9 close came before the dollar index firmed in New York.

After the bell

November 29: OPEC+ ministers meet on output policy. Reuters sources said the group agreed in principle to keep targets unchanged for the first quarter of 2027.

November 30: Cyber Monday and the last trading day of November.

December 1: ISM manufacturing index. Jensen Huang is scheduled for a closed-door interview with three House committees.

December 3: Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee.

December 4: November jobs report, 8:30 a.m. ET.

December 8 to 9: Federal Open Market Committee meeting.
