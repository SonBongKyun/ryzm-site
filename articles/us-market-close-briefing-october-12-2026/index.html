US Market Close Briefing | October 12, 2026

US Close | October 12, 2026

Stock markets were open. Bond markets were not. The S&P 500 was down 0.1% at 7,807.24, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.4% to 27,245.90, and the Russell 2000 dropped 0.4% to 2,794.48, while the Dow rose 0.4% to 51,850.55 for its third straight gain.

With the bond market closed for Columbus Day on SIFMA’s recommendation, the rate complex ran on quotes rather than prints. The 10-year held around 5.24% and the 30-year near 5.60%, both just below last week’s 24-year highs. Reuters flagged mortgage convexity hedging as another layer of selling pressure on top of record speculative shorts, and December hike odds sat near 85% in trading-desk models.

The won had its own day. Seoul closed at 1,335.1 per dollar, down 7.7 won, the strongest won in two years. Exporters kept converting record October dollar proceeds, with first-ten-days exports of $26.1 billion. The euro eased near $1.12.

Front-month WTI settled up 2.06% at $93.74, its third straight gain, after Houthi attacks on a Riyadh airport and a tanker incident off Oman. December Brent rose 1.8% to $106.65, back above $106 for the first time since September 29. Trump’s announced Russia-Ukraine energy ceasefire was rejected by Kyiv, and his 800,000-ton Russian diesel purchase drew a bipartisan Senate rebuke. Gold was down 0.6% at about $4,168.

Inside equities, the rotation was the trade. Salesforce gained on an analyst upgrade, energy caught the oil bid, and Meta added 3.4% to $742.96 on its Muse Wonder model launch. The AI hardware complex paid for it: Micron fell 3.1% to $996.80 on China’s CXMT memory-cell progress, Sandisk lost more than 6%, and Lumentum dropped nearly 10%. Bitcoin faded from near $84,200.

Editorial clarification — 14 October 2026 (KST): AP reports Micron’s 12 October closing decline at about 3.9%, rather than the 3.1% stated above. The original $996.80 figure is not verified as that session’s close. SanDisk’s 5.97% closing decline rounds to about 6%, rather than more than 6%. See the closing-price sources and scope below.

The won’s two-year high matters more than the Nasdaq’s 0.4% slide for the Korean open. It confirms the exporter dollar-selling flow that has been capping USD/KRW since July. But the memory selloff lands directly. Micron’s slide on CXMT progress and Sandisk’s drop are read-across to Korean DRAM names, while a 10-year at 5.24% leaves no discount-rate relief for growth multiples.

Next session: Tuesday brings August existing-home sales and the big-bank earnings start with Citigroup, JPMorgan, and Wells Fargo. The September CPI is scheduled for Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET, though the government shutdown has put the release calendar in question.

Sources and data scope

Source review: 14 October 2026 (KST). Equity observations below refer to the U.S. session of 12 October 2026; Korean FX refers to that date’s 15:30 KST daytime benchmark.

AP’s 12 October closing-index dispatch reports the S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq closing levels quoted above. The dispatch was published at 20:28 UTC. Reuters’ closing report, carried by MarketScreener, also documents that session and the Treasury-market holiday.

U.S. Treasury daily par-yield data records 5.24% for ten years and 5.60% for thirty years on 9 October, the preceding published observation. These should not be read as official 12 October closing yields. The BLS October calendar lists September CPI for 14 October at 08:30 ET; a scheduled release is not a released result.

Closing-price clarification: AP’s closing narrative reports Micron down about 3.9%, which conflicts with the 3.1% figure in the original body. Barchart’s historical SanDisk quotes show a 5.97% decline on 12 October. The original Micron price and the phrase “more than 6%” for SanDisk are not verified closing observations and require a separately identified intraday timestamp before reuse.
