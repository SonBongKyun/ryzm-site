US Market Close Briefing | October 13, 2026

US Close | October 13, 2026

The 4 p.m. ET close on Oct 13, 2026 looked flat and traded split. The S&P 500 closed at 7,805.45, down 0.02%. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.15% to 27,204.38. The Dow opened higher and gave it all back to finish at 51,670.10, down 0.35%. The Russell 2000 slipped 0.40% to 2,783.37. Breadth held up: 271 S&P members rose against 230 decliners.

Rates gave the first relief of the day. Treasury’s official CMT reference put the 10-year at 5.21%, down 3 bp from Friday, with the 2-year at 4.79%, down 1 bp. Bond trading had resumed after the Monday holiday, and the curve eased into Wednesday’s inflation data.

The won weakened to near 1,343 per dollar, up 8.9 won from Monday’s level. The VIX rose 0.46% to 15.28.

Commodities pulled in both directions. Brent’s front-month contract closed down 0.90% at $105.69 on Dow Jones Market Data, as the Russian diesel news met Houthi attacks in the Gulf. Front-month WTI prints diverged across providers, so the level stays out of the record. Gold held near $4,186 an ounce, down 0.11%. Bitcoin traded near $83,170, up 0.22%.

Earnings season opened with a split verdict. JPMorgan posted a rare miss, EPS of $5.57 against a $5.90 consensus, its first in 17 quarters, as fixed-income trading revenue fell 4% from a year earlier. Goldman Sachs ran the other way, with net revenue of $18.68 billion and equities trading up 72% to $6.44 billion. Jamie Dimon warned that financial pain would worsen if rates rose another percentage point, with traders pricing strong odds of a December hike.

The chip selloff crossed the Pacific. CXMT claimed a 4F² DRAM breakthrough and server memory before year-end, a claim Bloomberg called unsubstantiated. Samsung fell 7.4% and SK Hynix 10.1% in Seoul, dragging the KOSPI down 5.46% to its lowest close since late July.

What reaches Korea?

The CXMT claim lands directly on Korean memory supply chains. Seoul felt it before New York opened, and a 5.21% 10-year keeps discount-rate pressure on growth names.

Next session

September CPI is scheduled for Wednesday morning. WSJ-surveyed economists expect the monthly pace to accelerate to 0.6% from 0.4%, with the annual reading near 3.6%. A government shutdown has clouded the federal data calendar, so the release is not guaranteed. Bank of America and Morgan Stanley report Wednesday.
