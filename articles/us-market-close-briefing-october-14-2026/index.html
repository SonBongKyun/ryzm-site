US Market Close Briefing | October 14, 2026

US Close | October 14, 2026

Wednesday’s inflation print rewrote the policy calendar. September CPI rose 3.7% from a year earlier, above the 3.6% Wall Street Journal economists expected and up from 3.4% in August. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, also topped forecasts at 2.6%. Gasoline did the damage, up 9.1% on the month and 34% from a year ago.

The S&P 500 fell to 7,718.94, down 1.11% on the day, its first decline of at least 1% in more than 50 trading days. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 26,692.87, down 1.88%. The Russell 2000 closed at 2,729.53, down 1.93%, sliding into correction territory more than 10% below this year’s record. The Dow closed at 51,621.48, down 0.09%, cushioned by a defensive rotation.

Bonds whipsawed. The 10-year yield spiked above 5.32% right after the CPI release, then fell to 5.189% by the close, down 3 bp on the day and its lowest in nearly three weeks. The 2-year inched up 1 bp to 4.80%. Markets now price roughly a one-in-three chance of an October 28 hike, up from about 22% on Tuesday. The Fed’s own line stayed cautious. Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman said she felt no urgency for further action, telling an IMF meeting in Bangkok that the Fed needed time to understand how things evolve.

The dollar rose to its highest in over a year. The won firmed to 1,338 per dollar in Seoul trading, down 6.8 won on the day, as exporters sold dollars into the bid.

Commodities split along the policy channel. Front-month Brent settled at $106.06, up 0.35% on the day, after Houthi militants struck a building at Saudi Aramco. Front-month WTI also rose on the day, but provider prints across the $94 to $96 range diverged, so the level stays out of the record. Gold futures fell to $4,084.80, down 1.64% on the day, as real yields climbed, and bitcoin slipped toward $82,000.

Banks extended Tuesday’s slide. Bank of America fell 4.8% after profit disappointed even excluding a $1.2 billion fine. Morgan Stanley lost 2.75% despite higher quarterly profit. Credit drew a line under the AI buildout: CoreWeave’s 9.625% notes due 2032 traded at $79.50 on Wednesday, and the stock fell 15.5%. Oracle dropped 8.8%, Nebius 10.1%, Dell 11.3%, and the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 4.3% for a sixth straight session. Eight of eleven S&P sectors fell while health care and staples rose.

What reaches Korea?

The CPI print travels through the dollar and the discount rate. A 10-year near 5.19% with upside risk keeps pressure on growth names, and the chip complex now fights on two fronts: Chinese DRAM claims and a US reassessment of AI capex. The KOSPI closed down 0.44% at 6,008.81 with foreigners selling 1.18 trillion won.

Next session

September PPI and retail sales are due Thursday, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh speaks Friday at the IMF annual meeting. The question is whether the bond whipsaw marked exhaustion of the selloff or just a pause.
