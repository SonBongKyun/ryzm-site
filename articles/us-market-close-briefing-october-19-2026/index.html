US Market Close Briefing | October 19, 2026

The S&P 500 closed up 0.41% at 7,690.73. The Nasdaq 100 rose to 29,514.32, up 1.12%. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.11% to 26,430.89 while the Dow slipped 0.07% to 51,966.03 and the Russell 2000 added 0.25% to 2,728.30.

The rebound was narrow. Only 176 of 503 S&P 500 names advanced at the 3:59 p.m. ET snapshot, and technology was the lone sector with a positive average return. The equal-weight S&P 500 fell 0.32%. Consumer discretionary rose 1.78% and tech 1.36%, while utilities dropped 2.01% and real estate 1.16%.

Rates pushed higher into the equity bounce. The 10-year ended up 3.8 bp at 5.135%, its largest gain since October 2, while the 2-year added 2.5 bp to 4.759% and the 30-year 3.5 bp to 5.472%. Long-duration Treasuries fell 0.74%.

AI credit stayed the fault line. Amazon jumped 4.98% and Tesla 3.76% ahead of their reports this week, the first Mag 7 earnings of the season. CoreWeave fell 0.4% to $61.12 even after a Goldman Sachs upgrade to Buy, as derivatives markets kept pricing stress on its debt-funded data-center buildout.

The macro backdrop hardened. September CPI printed 3.7%, above expectations, and Barclays now expects Fed hikes in December and January. Traders priced a 17.2% chance of a hike at next week’s meeting. The NAHB housing index fell to 29, its lowest since June 2012, as mortgage rates tracked yields to multiyear highs.

Energy eased on policy. The December WTI contract, now the front-month, fell 0.5% to $98.54 after President Trump waived some Russia sanctions and tariffs on Friday and cut a diesel export deal. Brent for December delivery settled down 0.3% at $109.55. Diesel futures sit 6.5% below their recent record.

What reaches Korea

The chip rebound is the direct channel. A renewed AI-capex bid matters more to Korean memory and AI-server supply chains than the Nasdaq headline alone. The won also gives importers room, with USD/KRW down 1.1 won to 1,330.3, the won’s firmest since October 2024. The constraint is unchanged. A 5.135% 10-year keeps growth multiples under a hard discount rate, and Brent near $109.55 stays expensive for an energy importer.

Next session

Tesla and Meta report this week alongside more than 70 S&P 500 companies, the first real check on the AI spending narrative behind last week’s 4.5% Nasdaq rout. The Treasury auctions $13 billion of 20-year bonds on Wednesday.
